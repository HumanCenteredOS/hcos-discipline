# Post-Test Answer Key and Rationales

## The Future of AI May Feel Like One Relationship

**Version:** 1.1  
**Faculty / administrator use**

---

## Question 1

**Correct answer: B — Use task-specific capabilities with context-bound access and delegated authority.**

A continuous experience may be coordinated across several systems without granting every system broad access or authority. Task-specific boundaries preserve context, minimum-necessary information use, and accountability.

**Learning objective:** Differentiate the continuous experience from the distributed architecture underneath it.

---

## Question 2

**Correct answer: B — Capability describes what the system can do; delegated authority defines what it is permitted to do in a particular context.**

Technical ability does not create permission. Authority should be intentionally defined according to identity, role, purpose, context, scope, duration, and review requirements.

**Learning objective:** Describe why delegated authority matters in healthcare AI.

---

## Question 3

**Correct answer: C — Verify the source, context, current permission, and whether the preference remains accurate.**

A preference that was appropriate during one encounter may not represent permanent authorization across future settings. Governed memory preserves source, context, correction, expiration, and current consent.

**Learning objective:** Describe the role of governed memory and identify context-boundary risk.

---

## Question 4

**Correct answer: C — The consequence, uncertainty, reversibility, and professional responsibility associated with the action.**

Human review should be meaningful and proportional. Higher-consequence, uncertain, difficult-to-reverse, or professionally regulated actions generally require stronger review and clearer accountability.

**Learning objective:** Apply a practical stewardship lens before AI moves from information to action.

---

## Question 5

**Correct answer: B — The AI exceeded the defined authority boundary before required review.**

The system may be permitted to retrieve information and prepare a draft, but submission is a delegated transaction. The workflow must define when the action may occur and who must review it first.

**Learning objective:** Identify boundary risks involving authority and review.

---

## Question 6

**Correct answer: C — Map the people, systems, information, actions, authority boundaries, review points, and accountable owner.**

Workflow mapping makes the hidden architecture visible. It helps the organization distinguish retrieval, drafting, communication, decision, and action before expanding automation.

**Learning objective:** Apply the stewardship lens to an AI-supported healthcare workflow.

---

## Question 7

**Correct answer: C — Continuity should support people while preserving boundaries among identity, context, memory, authority, capability, and responsibility.**

The HCOS™ position does not reject continuity. It argues that continuity should reduce burden without collapsing the distinctions that protect dignity, privacy, judgment, safety, and accountability.

**Learning objective:** Integrate the central architectural message of the activity.
