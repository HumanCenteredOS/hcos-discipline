# Educational Scope and Disclaimer

## The Future of AI May Feel Like One Relationship

## Short Slide Version

> This activity provides conceptual education for healthcare professionals. It is not legal, regulatory, privacy, cybersecurity, billing, compliance, clinical, or technical implementation advice. Examples are illustrative and must be adapted to local policy, workflow, governance, patient needs, professional scope, and applicable law. Healthcare organizations and licensed professionals remain responsible for validation, safety, privacy, fairness, documentation, judgment, and accountability.

## Full Learner Version

This continuing education activity is intended to support professional learning, systems thinking, and discussion about the human-centered design and governance of artificial intelligence in healthcare.

The activity does not provide:

- patient-specific medical advice;
- legal or regulatory advice;
- privacy or cybersecurity advice;
- billing, coding, or reimbursement advice;
- compliance determinations;
- product validation;
- clinical implementation approval;
- or a substitute for organizational governance and professional judgment.

Examples are simplified and illustrative. They should not be interpreted as instructions to automate a particular healthcare function or to grant an AI system a particular level of access or authority.

Any real-world implementation should be evaluated according to the organization’s:

- patient population and care setting;
- clinical and operational workflows;
- privacy and security requirements;
- professional scopes of practice;
- informed-consent and communication requirements;
- quality and patient-safety processes;
- equity and nondiscrimination obligations;
- documentation standards;
- vendor and technology controls;
- applicable federal, state, and local law;
- and accredited-provider or institutional policies.

AI systems should not make patient-specific decisions or perform consequential healthcare actions solely because the technology is capable of doing so. The system must operate within defined identity, context, information, authority, review, escalation, correction, and accountability boundaries.

Healthcare organizations remain responsible for:

- selecting and validating appropriate tools;
- defining permitted use;
- monitoring performance;
- protecting patient information;
- preventing discriminatory or inequitable effects;
- preserving professional judgment;
- maintaining appropriate documentation;
- responding to errors and unintended consequences;
- and ensuring that patients and healthcare professionals have access to responsible human review.

## HCOS™ Scope Statement

HCOS™ means Human-Centered Operating Systems. It is an original conceptual lens that examines technology together with the surrounding workflow, policy, staffing, resources, incentives, leadership decisions, communication pathways, daily operations, and human burden.

The HCOS™ concepts presented in this activity are not an established regulatory framework, accreditation requirement, or scientific consensus. They are offered as a structured way to identify human-centered questions that may otherwise remain invisible during AI design and implementation.

## Forecast Statement

The activity presents an architectural forecast. It does not claim that the future described is certain or that every AI system will develop in the same way.

The forecast is used to support prospective thinking about what healthcare organizations may need to govern if people begin interacting with AI as one continuous relationship supported by many specialized systems.

## Use of Names, Scenarios, and Organizations

Unless specifically identified as a documented public example, names and scenarios are fictional or generalized for educational purposes. Similarity to a specific patient, clinician, organization, or event should not be inferred.
