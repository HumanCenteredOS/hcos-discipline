## Knowledge Check 1 — Coordinated Follow-Up

### Scenario

A healthcare AI coordinates follow-up across scheduling, pharmacy, and insurance systems.

### Question

What is the most important architectural safeguard?

A. Use the same AI model for every task.  
B. Allow all systems to access the complete patient record.  
C. Limit each capability to the identity, context, information, and authority needed for its task.  
D. Remove human review so the workflow remains continuous.

### Correct Answer

**C. Limit each capability to the identity, context, information, and authority needed for its task.**

### Explanation

A continuous experience should not mean unlimited access or authority. Scheduling, clinical review, pharmacy coordination, and payer transactions may require different information and permissions. Task-specific boundaries support privacy, safety, and accountability while still allowing the overall workflow to be coordinated.

### Learning Objective Alignment

- Differentiate a continuous AI experience from the distributed systems underneath it.
- Identify boundary risks across healthcare contexts.

## Knowledge Check 2 — Drafting Versus Sending

### Scenario

An AI drafts a patient message about missing laboratory work and then sends the message automatically.

### Question

What is the most important governance concern?

A. The AI should use more conversational language.  
B. Sending is a delegated action that requires defined identity, authority, and review.  
C. The AI should store every prior patient message.  
D. The message should be sent through more than one communication channel.

### Correct Answer

**B. Sending is a delegated action that requires defined identity, authority, and review.**

### Explanation

Drafting prepares communication. Sending performs communication on behalf of a clinician, pharmacy, or organization. The system must have clearly defined authority, an appropriate review threshold, and a pathway for handling uncertainty or patient response.

### Learning Objective Alignment

- Describe why healthcare AI requires delegated authority.
- Apply a stewardship lens as AI moves from information toward action.

## Knowledge Check 3 — Authority Before Review

### Scenario

An AI reviews a patient record, drafts an authorization, messages the prescriber, and submits the request before the pharmacist completes the clinical review.

### Question

What is the primary governance problem?

A. The AI used too many systems.  
B. The AI exceeded delegated authority before the required review threshold was met.  
C. The pharmacist should not participate in the workflow.  
D. All prior authorizations should be fully automated.

### Correct Answer

**B. The AI exceeded delegated authority before the required review threshold was met.**

### Explanation

Capability is not permission. Retrieval and drafting may be appropriate before professional review, but submission is a delegated transaction that may affect access to care. The workflow should define which actions may occur before review and which require confirmation by an authorized person or approved governance pathway.

### Learning Objective Alignment

- Identify authority and accountability boundaries.
- Apply the practical review method to a healthcare workflow.
