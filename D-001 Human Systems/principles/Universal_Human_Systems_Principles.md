# Universal Human Systems Principles

## Principle 1 — Every Organization Is a Human System

Organizations are not merely structures, technologies, or policies. They are living networks of people, relationships, responsibilities, decisions, and meaning.

## Principle 2 — Every Human System Exists to Serve People

Systems should be evaluated according to how well they protect dignity, reduce unnecessary suffering, and support flourishing.

## Principle 3 — System Conditions Shape Human Behavior

Workload, workflow, policies, metrics, resources, leadership decisions, daily operations, and recovery time influence what people can realistically do.

## Principle 4 — Human Experience Is Evidence

The experiences of people closest to the work are legitimate evidence of system conditions, while still requiring disciplined interpretation.

## Principle 5 — Pressure Moves Through Systems

Pressure may be created, transferred, absorbed, hidden, normalized, or reduced.

## Principle 6 — Authority and Responsibility Should Align

People should not be held responsible for outcomes when they lack the authority, information, access, resources, or support needed to influence them.

## Principle 7 — Healthy Systems Preserve Human Protections

Wisdom, compassion, presence, and meaning are not optional extras. They are conditions that support healthy judgment and sustainable work.

## Principle 8 — Evidence and Interpretation Should Remain Distinguishable

Healthy systems preserve the difference between what was observed, how it was interpreted, and what is recommended.

## Principle 9 — Healthy Systems Learn

Systems should continuously evaluate outcomes, unintended consequences, uncertainty, and opportunities to improve.

## Principle 10 — Healthy Systems Steward the Future

Decisions should account for long-term human consequences, institutional memory, resilience, and the needs of those who come after.
