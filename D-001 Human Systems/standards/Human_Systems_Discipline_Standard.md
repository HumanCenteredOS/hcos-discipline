# HCOS™ Human Systems Discipline Standard

## Standard Statement

HCOS assessments should examine systems rather than attributing complex outcomes to individuals without sufficient evidence.

## Required Practices

- Begin with system purpose.
- Include the perspectives of people closest to the work.
- Distinguish evidence from interpretation.
- Identify who holds authority and who carries consequences.
- Examine burden transfer.
- Preserve uncertainty.
- Avoid unnecessary personal identification.
- Consider both operational and human outcomes.
- Document limitations.
- Include a learning and review process.

## Prohibited Practices

- unsupported judgments about individual intent,
- collapsing multidimensional system health into one score without justification,
- treating qualitative evidence as inherently inferior,
- treating rhetoric as verified fact,
- recommending automation before understanding the workflow,
- or shifting responsibility to people without matching authority.
