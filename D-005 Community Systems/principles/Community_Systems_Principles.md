# Community Systems Principles

## Principle 1 — Communities Are Interdependent Human Systems

Community outcomes emerge from interactions among institutions, infrastructure, environment, public policy, markets, and residents.

## Principle 2 — People Experience Systems as One Life

Residents do not experience healthcare, education, transportation, insurance, housing, and employment as separate disciplines. They experience their combined effects.

## Principle 3 — Institutional Solutions Can Transfer Burden

An organization may improve its own performance while shifting cost, work, risk, delay, or responsibility elsewhere.

## Principle 4 — Community Risk Is Cumulative

Multiple moderate pressures may combine into serious instability even when no single indicator appears catastrophic.

## Principle 5 — Protections Must Function in Practice

A protection that exists only on paper, is inaccessible, or excludes affected populations is not a complete protection.

## Principle 6 — Community Decisions Require Meaningful Participation

People who experience the consequences should have meaningful opportunities to contribute evidence and perspective.

## Principle 7 — Accountability Must Cross Boundaries

When outcomes are produced by several systems, governance should not allow responsibility to disappear between institutions.

## Principle 8 — Resilience Should Not Depend on Repeated Human Sacrifice

A community is not resilient merely because the same people repeatedly compensate for system weaknesses.

## Principle 9 — Public Rhetoric Can Contain Human Meaning

Provocative or imprecise claims may still signal legitimate distress, burden, distrust, or missing protections.

## Principle 10 — Healthy Communities Learn Across Systems

Community learning should connect evidence, experience, decisions, consequences, recovery, and future stewardship.
