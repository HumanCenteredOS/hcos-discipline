# HCOS™ Community Systems Interdependency Map

**Instrument ID:** HCOS-CS-001  
**Suite:** HCOS™ Community Systems Assessment Suite  
**Status:** Draft  
**Steward:** Sophia Care Alliance™  

## Purpose

Map the major systems supporting a community and identify how they depend on one another.

## Central Question

> How is the community designed to function, and which dependencies are most consequential?

## Intended Use

Use this instrument during community assessment, public-sector review, nonprofit collaboration, policy analysis, research, or cross-system planning when the central question requires structured examination of community systems and human consequences.

## Objectives

- Identify major community systems and institutions.
- Document the services or protections each system provides.
- Map critical dependencies and shared resources.
- Identify single points of failure and hidden dependencies.
- Show where one system's disruption may affect another.

## Suggested Evidence

- Administrative and service data
- Policies and plans
- Public records
- Scientific or technical studies
- Interviews and listening sessions
- Resident experiences
- Community surveys
- Geographic or access data
- De-identified case examples
- Existing performance measures

## Core Domains

- Healthcare and transportation
- Education and workforce recruitment
- Agriculture and water quality
- Weather and insurance availability
- Broadband and healthcare access

## Rating Guidance

Use ratings only when useful.

| Rating | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Harmful, absent, or severely strained |
| 2 | Unreliable and dependent on human compensation |
| 3 | Functional but creating preventable burden |
| 4 | Supportive and generally reliable |
| 5 | Strong, equitable, resilient, and continuously learning |

Ratings must never stand alone. Include evidence and rationale.

## Expected Output

Produce a concise output containing:

- key findings,
- supporting evidence,
- human consequences,
- protection gaps,
- governance implications,
- uncertainty,
- and recommended next questions or actions.

## Safeguards

Do not:

- infer individual intent without evidence,
- treat rhetoric as verified fact,
- dismiss lived experience because it is qualitative,
- collapse multiple dimensions into one score too early,
- recommend action without considering burden transfer,
- or identify individuals unnecessarily.

## Validation Questions

- Did the instrument reveal something that existing metrics missed?
- Were affected people meaningfully represented?
- Were evidence and interpretation distinguishable?
- Were cross-system effects visible?
- Did the review identify who carries the consequences?
- Did the output support a clearer decision?
